About Eonity

AI that structures information, with people in charge.

Eonity develops open-source tools that apply AI to structuring information (digital assets, websites and documents) while a person observes, reviews, accepts and suggests.

Updated November 25, 2026

Much of the work around information is giving it structure: describing a collection, organizing a website, keeping a long document consistent and translated. AI can now carry out much of that work. Eonity’s tools let it do so while a person stays at the center: the AI proposes and makes the changes; people watch, review, accept them or suggest others, and nothing is applied without them.

Where it comes from

Eonity is new; the ideas behind it are not. Its tools distill many years of its founder’s work on decoupled content management, a concept the founder coined, and on digital asset management with vaults, first built to deliver content through CDNs. What Eonity adds is AI that works on that structure, with a person at the center.

Three tools, one approach

Each tool works on one kind of information and lets the AI act only on the part you choose.

Tool Information What the AI does What you do
TYDAL Digital assets: images, documents, datasets, code Proposes titles, descriptions and tags Approve, edit or reject each suggestion
TYKERF Websites Changes the part of a page you point at Watch, accept, or undo step by step
TYSET (coming soon) Documents: MIF (DITA planned) Reviews, corrects and translates one section at a time Accept, change or undo each proposal

Why each tool exists

TYDAL came first. Collections of images, documents and data are only useful when they are well described, and few teams have the time to describe them. TYDAL lets each collection define its own fields, has the AI propose the descriptions, and shares chosen selections, called Vaults, with people, applications and AI assistants. About TYDAL explains its model, and FullFrame, a photography exhibition application, shows how other applications build on it.

TYKERF applies the same approach to websites. Every page is made of named blocks: you point at one, say what should change, and the AI changes that block and nothing else, while every step can be undone. TYKERF Community Edition is free and open source, and works as a plugin in Claude.

TYSET exists because structured desktop publishing has yet to benefit from AI. It will open MIF documents, with DITA planned, and keep their structure and layout, while the AI reviews, corrects and translates one section at a time.

How the AI comes in

In two ways. Each tool has its own built-in assistant; in TYKERF it is called Kaity. And through MCP, an open standard for connecting AI assistants to tools, Claude and other assistants receive the same actions the tools use themselves. For Claude, Eonity publishes plugins. Either way, the person stays at the center.

The names

The TY in each name marks the tools as one family. The DAL in TYDAL stands for Digital Asset Layer. A kerf is the cut a saw leaves: TYKERF borrows the way chip designers slice a floor plan into rows and columns, and uses it to cut web pages into named blocks.

Open source

Eonity’s tools are open source under the Apache License 2.0. The code lives in the eonity-org organization on GitHub. They are built for developers and early adopters; services, support and implementation can support their development.
